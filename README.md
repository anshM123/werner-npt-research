# Werner states at the NPT endpoint: multi-copy undistillability results

Ansh Mishra and Aryan Senthilkumar

This repository collects our results, as of 2 November 2026, on the question of whether NPT bound entanglement exists.
That is Problem 4 of Horodecki, Rudnicki and Życzkowski, PRX Quantum 3, 010101 (2022). The standard route to it is the
qutrit Werner state ρ ∝ I − F/2: is it undistillable from every number of copies? At the endpoint α = 1/2, n-copy
undistillability is equivalent (Costa Rico; Qi–Gupur–Wu–Guo; Song–Chen) to

    q_n(C) = Σ_J (−1/2)^{|J|} ‖Tr_J C‖_2² ≥ 0   for every operator C of rank at most two on (C^d)^{⊗n}.

**This repository does not solve the problem.** The all-copy statement is still open: we have neither a proof for
every n nor a counterexample. The newest result is computer-assisted: **at four copies, q_4(C) ≥ 0 for every Hermitian operator
C of rank at most two** (see [`four-replica-notes/FOUR_COPY_THEOREMS.md`](four-replica-notes/FOUR_COPY_THEOREMS.md)).
By the Re/Im splitting of Wu and Zou, this extends to every rank-two C whose range and co-range lie in one plane, for
example every normal C. The full four-copy endpoint, for rank-two C whose range and co-range are different planes, is
still open. In our exploratory searches (local optimisation; that code is not part of this
repository) the minimum of q_n over rank-two C at α = 1/2 was always 0, and never negative, for n ≤ 11 copies at
d = 3, 7 at d = 4, 6 at d = 5 and 5 at d = 6. The scripted searches here cover the bosonic sector for n ≤ 7 and
local runs at three copies. What follows are partial results, each with its status.

The one-copy reductions can have rank three at every copy, and the lowest thick critical point we found at two copies, R = (9 − √41)/20, lies in this class. The proof is short: an exact identity, a lemma on Pauli coefficients for qubit operators, a Schur-complement case split, and the inequality q_n(C) ≥ q_n(P_V C P_V) for range in V (Tomar's Lemma 4.8 argument). New for n ≥ 4 when A or B has planes at two or more copies; the smaller cases overlap Tomar's every-copy classes. An extension leaves one copy unconstrained. Then q_n ≥ 0 when both diagonal blocks have rank at most one (C of rank up to six), and so for every rank-two C whose range is spanned by one vector of C^3 ⊗ A and one of C^3 ⊗ B. Its key step is a new matrix inequality, |B_1(Z,W)|² ≤ ψ(Z)ψ(W) with ψ(Z) = ‖Z‖₁² − ½|Tr Z|². With two free copies the same rank-one statements follow from Tomar's computer-assisted three-copy theorem in arbitrary dimensions. In general, f free copies reduce to the endpoint for f + 1 copies, one of them of arbitrary dimension. Grouping the sector copies (at most one plane per side in each group) makes the reduction an exact identity for every C. So the endpoint holds for every rank-two C in the class when f + max(a, b, 1) ≤ 3 (Theorem C). This includes a four-copy class thick at every copy, and the shared-tail cases of Tomar's §8 when |T_0 ∩ T_1| + max(|T_0 \ T_1|, |T_1 \ T_0|, 1) ≤ 3. With three sectors (all lines) the same collapse works when m − g is even, which gives every rank-two C on C^3 ⊗ span{1…1, 2…2, 3…3} for every n. Collapsing blocks one at a time (Theorem D: free copies, two-sector groups and three-sector blocks, weights adding up to at most 3) gives, for every n, classes thick at every copy that use only the two-copy theorem, for example C^3 ⊗ span{1^{n−1}, 2^{n−1}, 3^{n−1}} for even n; it is enough that the range lies in such a product, with any co-range. Theorem C's own new cases (f ≥ 1) sit on at most five thick copies, since its (1,1) copies are thin. With one free copy and one excitation on m ≥ 3 copies, Theorem E covers ranges in C^3 ⊗ span{c, e} (c entangled), and the rest of that class reduces to one explicit inequality (⋆), with constant 2^m − 2 needed. | [`compression-notes/`](compression-notes) | A compression conjecture that would give every n: some copy and some direction w with q_n(C) ≥ q_n(C compressed to w^⊥). It comes with an exact formula and adversarial evidence up to five copies. Averages of contractions that do not depend on C can only certify t ≤ 1/d, the PPT range. The projector case is an exact monogamy relation for a purifying qubit, for every n. Translation-invariant MPS filters are described by a completely positive transfer channel; a search up to 33 copies finds no negative value. An explicit witness shows that two-pair Werner mixtures with a positive margin can be activated by one Werner copy. Operators whose range lies in a product of planes over two-copy blocks are nonnegative for every n and every rank. This gives another every-n class, thick at every copy. Given n − 1 copies, a filter on one copy can lower the form in at most one direction, and at a first zero below the endpoint the filter form has kernel exactly the identity. On the code space of a pure qutrit code with n = 2d − 1 the form is exactly A_n‖x‖² + B_n|Tr x|²; for the five-qutrit code this gives q_5 ≥ (71/288)‖C‖² for every operator whose range lies in the code space (co-range arbitrary). So products of planes over blocks of at most three copies and five-qutrit code spaces over five-copy blocks give an every-rank class with 3-dimensional factors. Finally, a reduction through cones of bond operators: if every local filtering of m + 1 Werner copies into dimension 3^m lies in the cone generated by filterings of m copies (KL_m), then the endpoint holds for every n on operators of bond dimension at most 3^m; for 2m copies, output dimension 2·3^(m−1) is enough. So a statement about 6 × 6 filterings of three copies would give the four-copy endpoint. Compressing all copies but one always gives a 6 × 6 filtering, so the endpoint for every n would follow from one such statement per number of copies (each implied by KL_m for m ≥ 2); the analogous one-copy statement at dimension 6 looks false, which matches the dimension bound. The hypotheses are numerically supported for m = 1, and under test for m = 2. Exact positive spectra for the antisymmetric subspace of two copies (smallest eigenvalue 1/4) and for the qutrit [[3,1,2]] code (1/2) give every-n, every-rank classes containing operators thick at every copy, with ranges of dimension up to 3^(n/2). The compressions to the antisymmetric subspace, the [[3,1,2]], [[4,2,2]] and [[5,3,2]] code spaces and the five-qutrit code space are even separable (explicit decompositions, the Gurvits–Barnum ball on syndrome blocks, or exact certificates by stabiliser product states). So a block of two, three, four or five copies where the range lies in such a subspace can be removed. Iterating, the endpoint holds for every n, with no unproven input, whenever the range lies in a product of such blocks and planes with at most three copies left free; this includes cases with up to eight copies. For any qutrit stabiliser code, the partial-transpose spectrum of the compression is an exact weight sum over cosets of the stabiliser group; the codes [[b, b−2, 2]] give PPT blocks exactly for b ≤ 6. A fixed-dimension sufficient condition: if every 3 × 3 local filtering of any number of Werner copies lies in the cone generated by filterings of one copy (Conjecture F3), the endpoint holds for every n; F3 passes adversarial tests at three and four copies. Two Werner copies have Schmidt number 2, and no one-step induction by local congruences of F_2 ⊗ ρ exists (a numerical dual certificate). A rigorous floating-point check certifies three links of the 6 × 6 chain (NPT filterings of three and four copies lie in the cone generated by filterings of two; the two three-copy ones are instances of the statement that would give four copies) and F3 at five NPT targets with three to six copies. The one-copy cone is generated by filterings alone, and its one-filtering form is equivalent to a binary merger of two filterings; adversarial tests find no counterexample, four NPT two-filtering sums are certified in the one-filtering form, and a normal-form rule produces the merged filtering on interior targets (it fails next to the boundary). | Propositions proved; the conjectures (QC, KL_m, F3, one-sided separability) and the large-n searches are numerical |

| [`single-excitation-range/`](single-excitation-range) | Rank-two operators whose range lies in the single excitations over a product vector and whose co-range has at most two excitations: q_n ≥ 0 for every n. ## What is open

`OBSTRUCTIONS.md` lists the exact equivalent forms of the every-n statement and the reasons the natural approaches fail.

- The endpoint for every n. `compression-notes/` states a compression conjecture that would give it, with evidence up to five copies.
  `orbit-notes/` reduces it to a local statement on squeeze orbits (no thick squeeze-orbit minimum), supported numerically for n ≤ 7.
  Every one-copy version of that statement is false at three copies, and so are the versions restricted to the
  local-support torus or to the weak local planes (explicit three-copy critical points). The Levi form alone does not settle the two-sided
  version: at a two-copy critical point with positive Levi form the descent comes from a curvature term. On the whole
  rank-two variety, adversarial searches at two and three copies find no thick local minimum, and the lowest thick
  critical points have closed-form values ((9 − √41)/20 at two copies; (67 − √2329)/216 at three), all saddles.
  `two-sector-class/` proves the endpoint for every n when the range lies in two product subspaces that are orthogonal
  at every copy (any co-range). The two-copy point lies in that class. Its collapse identities (Theorems C and D) also
  give every-n classes thick at every copy: one side of C in a product of repetition codes, two-sector groups and free
  copies with weights adding up to at most 3. That route has reached its limit. Even repetition blocks cost two copies,
  and the first case outside it is one free copy times a one-excitation block with three planes,
  C^3 ⊗ (K⊗K⊗K ⊕ C e⊗e⊗e), at four copies. Read as LOCC, the collapse maps m Werner copies to a weakly NPT two-qutrit
  state ρ_m whose undistillability for every number of copies would already give NPT bound entanglement.
- The endpoint for every n, even restricted to operators whose range and co-range lie in one plane. This restriction is
  in fact equivalent to the full problem: by `support-and-projector-notes/` (Proposition 4), a negative rank-two
  witness on n copies gives a negative rank-two orthogonal projector on more copies. Section 4 of
  `three-copy-notes/NOTES.md` explains why the most natural inductive route fails.
- The endpoint for every n on the bosonic sector (range and co-range in Sym^n(C^3)). Section 4 of
  `bosonic-sector-notes/NOTES.md` isolates the remaining obstruction: interference between occupation blocks on one
  diagonal.
- Four copies at the exact endpoint. Tomar proves α ≤ 0.4979 for qutrits. `contraction-notes/` covers one class
  of operators that are thick at a copy. Section 9 of `compression-notes/NOTES.md` reduces it to a three-copy
  statement: every 6 × 6 local filtering of three Werner copies lies in the cone generated by filterings of two
  (two NPT instances are certified rigorously).
  Section 10 of the same notes proves the four-copy endpoint for every operator whose range at some pair of copies
  lies in a local-unitary image of the antisymmetric subspace, or at some triple in the [[3,1,2]] code space.
  `four-replica-notes/` gives numerical four-copy evidence for Hermitian rank-two operators: SDP lower bounds that
  match the sharp margin at 13 ratios. It gives exact rational certificates (computer-assisted) for every
  Hermitian rank-two operator, so the four-copy endpoint holds on that class and, by the Re/Im splitting, on every
  rank-two C with range and co-range in one plane; the remaining C are open. `two-sector-class/` (Theorem C, given
  Tomar's three-copy theorem in arbitrary dimensions) proves it for every rank-two C with range and co-range in
  C^9 ⊗ (K_3 ⊗ e_4 ⊕ e_3 ⊗ K_4), where K_3 and K_4 are planes, e_3 ⊥ K_3 and e_4 ⊥ K_4; generic members are thick at
  every copy.
- Tomar's two-copy constant 1/4 for operators whose range and co-range differ. We prove 1/37 in general, and the
  sharp constant for the plane class.
