# Werner states at the NPT endpoint: multi-copy undistillability results

Ansh Mishra and Aryan Senthilkumar

This repository collects our results, as of 2 November 2026, on the question of whether NPT bound entanglement exists.
That is Problem 4 of Horodecki, Rudnicki and Życzkowski, PRX Quantum 3, 010101 (2022). The standard route to it is the
qutrit Werner state ρ ∝ I − F/2: is it undistillable from every number of copies? At the endpoint α = 1/2, n-copy
undistillability is equivalent (Costa Rico; Qi–Gupur–Wu–Guo; Song–Chen) to

    q_n(C) = Σ_J (−1/2)^{|J|} ‖Tr_J C‖_2² ≥ 0   for every operator C of rank at most two on (C^d)^{⊗n}.

**This repository does not solve the problem.** The all-copy statement is still open: we have neither a proof for
every n nor a counterexample. The newest result is computer-assisted: **at four copies, q_4(C) ≥ 0 for every Hermitian operator
C of rank at most two** (see [`four-replica-notes/FOUR_COPY_THEOREMS.md`](four-replica-notes/FOUR_COPY_THEOREMS.md)).
By the Re/Im splitting of Wu and Zou, this extends to every rank-two C whose range and co-range lie in one plane, for
example every normal C. The full four-copy endpoint, for rank-two C whose range and co-range are different planes, is
still open. In our exploratory searches (local optimisation; that code is not part of this
repository) the minimum of q_n over rank-two C at α = 1/2 was always 0, and never negative, for n ≤ 11 copies at
d = 3, 7 at d = 4, 6 at d = 5 and 5 at d = 6. The scripted searches here cover the bosonic sector for n ≤ 7 and
local runs at three copies. What follows are partial results, each with its status.

A remark shows that crossed Cauchy–Schwarz is not tensor-stable. | Identities and diagonal theorem proved; general margin conjectured |
| [`orbit-notes/`](orbit-notes) | An every-n reduction through orbits of local filters. If no thick rank-two operator is a local minimum of the endpoint form on its own squeeze orbit X ↦ GXG (G = G_1 ⊗ … ⊗ G_n positive definite), then EP_{n−1} implies EP_n (short proof). Numerically, descent along squeeze orbits always runs to the boundary for n ≤ 7, at every Werner parameter tested up to t ≈ 0.95 (the property fails for large t at odd n, but the endpoint t = 1/2 is well inside), and ends at the conjectured minimum. Thick critical points found for n ≤ 5 are all saddles. Their lowest values are of order 2^{−n}, and there the descent comes mostly from coupling between copies. At three copies some thick critical points cannot be lowered by squeezing any single copy, yet pairs of copies still descend, so the property is not a one-copy statement. Exact formulas for the second variation are included. The property fails for some other invariant forms, so a proof must use the Werner structure. | Reduction proved; the orbit property is conjectured |
| [`contraction-notes/`](contraction-notes) | Passing the endpoint from n − 1 to n copies through contractions. An exact identity shows that an "aligned" class of rank-two operators (common backbone on the other copies, arbitrary tails) is nonnegative at n copies whenever n − 1 copies are. Nesting it gives an explicit class, thick at every copy, on which the endpoint inequality holds for every n with no unproven input. The zeros P ⊗ |a⟩⟨b| have nonnegative second variation for every n, given n − 1. A natural site-contraction inequality is false (an explicit three-copy operator). | Identity, nested class and second-variation statement proved; the counterexample is numerical |
| [`two-sector-class/`](two-sector-class) | Rank-two operators whose range (or co-range; the other side arbitrary) lies in A ⊕ B, where A = ⊗A_s and B = ⊗B_s are product subspaces with A_s ⊥ B_s at every copy (for example a product of planes plus one product vector orthogonal to it at every copy): q_n ≥ 0 for every n. The one-copy reductions can have rank three at every copy, and the lowest thick critical point we found at two copies, R = (9 − √41)/20, lies in this class. The proof is short: an exact identity, a lemma on Pauli coefficients for qubit operators, a Schur-complement case split, and the inequality q_n(C) ≥ q_n(P_V C P_V) for range in V (Tomar's Lemma 4.8 argument). New for n ≥ 4 when A or B has planes at two or more copies; the smaller cases overlap Tomar's every-copy classes. | Proved; independently re-derived and checked; not refereed |
| [`compression-notes/`](compression-notes) | A compression conjecture that would give every n: some copy and some direction w with q_n(C) ≥ q_n(C compressed to w^⊥). It comes with an exact formula and adversarial evidence up to five copies. Averages of contractions that do not depend on C can only certify t ≤ 1/d, the PPT range. The projector case is an exact monogamy relation for a purifying qubit, for every n. Translation-invariant MPS filters are described by a completely positive transfer channel; a search up to 33 copies finds no negative value. An explicit witness shows that two-pair Werner mixtures with a positive margin can be activated by one Werner copy. Operators whose range lies in a product of planes over two-copy blocks are nonnegative for every n and every rank. This gives another every-n class, thick at every copy. Given n − 1 copies, a filter on one copy can lower the form in at most one direction, and at a first zero below the endpoint the filter form has kernel exactly the identity. On the code space of a pure qutrit code with n = 2d − 1 the form is exactly A_n‖x‖² + B_n|Tr x|²; for the five-qutrit code this gives q_5 ≥ (71/288)‖C‖² for every operator whose range lies in the code space (co-range arbitrary). So products of planes over blocks of at most three copies and five-qutrit code spaces over five-copy blocks give an every-rank class with 3-dimensional factors. Finally, a reduction through cones of bond operators: if every local filtering of m + 1 Werner copies into dimension 3^m lies in the cone generated by filterings of m copies (KL_m), then the endpoint holds for every n on operators of bond dimension at most 3^m; for 2m copies, output dimension 2·3^(m−1) is enough. So a statement about 6 × 6 filterings of three copies would give the four-copy endpoint. Compressing all copies but one always gives a 6 × 6 filtering, so the endpoint for every n would follow from one such statement per number of copies (each implied by KL_m for m ≥ 2); the analogous one-copy statement at dimension 6 looks false, which matches the dimension bound. The hypotheses are numerically supported for m = 1, and under test for m = 2. Exact positive spectra for the antisymmetric subspace of two copies (smallest eigenvalue 1/4) and for the qutrit [[3,1,2]] code (1/2) give every-n, every-rank classes containing operators thick at every copy, with ranges of dimension up to 3^(n/2). The compressions to the antisymmetric subspace, the [[3,1,2]], [[4,2,2]] and [[5,3,2]] code spaces and the five-qutrit code space are even separable (explicit decompositions, the Gurvits–Barnum ball on syndrome blocks, or exact certificates by stabiliser product states). So a block of two, three, four or five copies where the range lies in such a subspace can be removed. Iterating, the endpoint holds for every n, with no unproven input, whenever the range lies in a product of such blocks and planes with at most three copies left free; this includes cases with up to eight copies. For any qutrit stabiliser code, the partial-transpose spectrum of the compression is an exact weight sum over cosets of the stabiliser group; the codes [[b, b−2, 2]] give PPT blocks exactly for b ≤ 6. A fixed-dimension sufficient condition: if every 3 × 3 local filtering of any number of Werner copies lies in the cone generated by filterings of one copy (Conjecture F3), the endpoint holds for every n; F3 passes adversarial tests at three and four copies. Two Werner copies have Schmidt number 2, and no one-step induction by local congruences of F_2 ⊗ ρ exists (a numerical dual certificate). A rigorous floating-point check certifies three links of the 6 × 6 chain (NPT filterings of three and four copies lie in the cone generated by filterings of two; the two three-copy ones are instances of the statement that would give four copies) and F3 at five NPT targets with three to six copies. The one-copy cone is generated by filterings alone, and its one-filtering form is equivalent to a binary merger of two filterings; adversarial tests find no counterexample, four NPT two-filtering sums are certified in the one-filtering form, and a normal-form rule produces the merged filtering on interior targets (it fails next to the boundary). | Propositions proved; the conjectures (QC, KL_m, F3, one-sided separability) and the large-n searches are numerical |
| [`four-replica-notes/`](four-replica-notes) | Semidefinite relaxations with four replicas at the endpoint. For rank-two projectors, a one-letter relaxation with a covariance (realignment) block is exact up to four copies but not at five. A two-letter relaxation uses that the letters are pure and orthogonal. For Hermitian rank-two operators at four copies it matches the conjectured sharp margin 2^{−n}(σ₁ − σ₂)² on a grid of ratios, and a single lifted certificate covers all ratios at two, three and four copies (numerically). **Exact rational certificates (computer-assisted) prove the four-copy endpoint for every Hermitian rank-two operator: q_4(aP_u + bP_v) ≥ 0 for all real a, b and orthonormal u, v in (C³)^⊗4** (one matrix-valued certificate for all ratios, plus separate ones for P_u ± P_v; re-checked independently at exact true points). The opposite-sign case needed a new zero family, qubit spin flips: on qubit subspaces q_n is 2^n times the weight on Pauli strings acting on every copy (proved). An exact identity shows the covariance block adds nothing to Tomar's relaxation for general C. The folder also has an exclusive-click reformulation of the endpoint on the pair-traceless slice (FO1, numerically exact up to six copies), and explicit failures of the natural four-copy versions of the three-copy proofs of Wu–Zou and Costa Rico. | Four copies, every Hermitian rank-two C, and every rank-two C with range and co-range in one plane: proved (computer-assisted, exact certificates; not refereed); qubit-sector lemma proved; the rest numerical |

Each folder has its own README or notes, the LaTeX source and PDF where there is a paper, and verification scripts.
The scripts need Python 3 with NumPy and SciPy. copy-amplification also needs SymPy and Matplotlib, and two of its
scripts need PyTorch.

## What is open

- The endpoint for every n. `compression-notes/` states a compression conjecture that would give it, with evidence up to five copies.
  `orbit-notes/` reduces it to a local statement on squeeze orbits (no thick squeeze-orbit minimum), supported numerically for n ≤ 7.
- The endpoint for every n, even restricted to operators whose range and co-range lie in one plane. Section 4 of
  `three-copy-notes/NOTES.md` explains why the most natural inductive route fails.
- The endpoint for every n on the bosonic sector (range and co-range in Sym^n(C^3)). Section 4 of
  `bosonic-sector-notes/NOTES.md` isolates the remaining obstruction: interference between occupation blocks on one
  diagonal.
- Four copies at the exact endpoint. Tomar proves α ≤ 0.4979 for qutrits. `contraction-notes/` covers one class
  of operators that are thick at a copy. Section 9 of `compression-notes/NOTES.md` reduces it to a three-copy
  statement: every 6 × 6 local filtering of three Werner copies lies in the cone generated by filterings of two
  (two NPT instances are certified rigorously).
  Section 10 of the same notes proves the four-copy endpoint for every operator whose range at some pair of copies
  lies in a local-unitary image of the antisymmetric subspace, or at some triple in the [[3,1,2]] code space.
  `four-replica-notes/` gives numerical four-copy evidence for Hermitian rank-two operators: SDP lower bounds that
  match the sharp margin at 13 ratios. It gives exact rational certificates (computer-assisted) for every
  Hermitian rank-two operator, so the four-copy endpoint holds on that class and, by the Re/Im splitting, on every
  rank-two C with range and co-range in one plane; the remaining C are open.
- Tomar's two-copy constant 1/4 for operators whose range and co-range differ. We prove 1/37 in general, and the
  sharp constant for the plane class.
