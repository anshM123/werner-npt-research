# What an every-n proof has to get past

Ansh Mishra and Aryan Senthilkumar, November 2026.

This page collects, in one place, the equivalent forms of the every-n endpoint and the exact reasons why the natural
approaches fail. It is meant to save time for anyone attacking the problem. Each item says where it is proved or
checked.

## Equivalent forms (all exact)

EP_N means q_N(C) ≥ 0 for every C of rank at most two on (C^3)^{⊗N}, where q_N(C) = Σ_J (−1/2)^{|J|} ‖Tr_J C‖².

1. **Map form.** EP_N holds iff Φ^{⊗N} is 2-positive, where Φ(X) = Tr(X)I − X/2 is the Terhal–Horodecki
   Schmidt-number-2 map. Φ is completely copositive: its partially transposed Choi matrix is W = I − F/2 > 0.
2. **Crossed Cauchy–Schwarz.** EP_N holds iff, for all vectors (orthogonality is not needed),

       |⟨x1 ⊗ y2|W_N|x2 ⊗ y1⟩|² ≤ ⟨x1 ⊗ y1|W_N|x1 ⊗ y1⟩ ⟨x2 ⊗ y2|W_N|x2 ⊗ y2⟩,   W_N = W^{⊗N}.

3. **Projectors** (`support-and-projector-notes/`, Proposition 4). EP for every N holds iff it holds for every rank-two
   orthogonal projector. Equivalently, the Hermitian, the PSD and the one-plane cases each carry the whole difficulty.
4. **Growth** (`support-and-projector-notes/`, Proposition 5 and Corollary 6). Let ρ_N be the supremum of the ratio
   in 2. Then ρ_{N+M} ≥ ρ_N ρ_M, so EP for every N holds iff ρ_N = e^{o(N)}. Any N-independent bound would do.
5. **Peeling one copy.** Write W = I/2 + P^A, with P^A the antisymmetric projector of the last copy. The I/2 part is
   controlled exactly by EP_N. The whole difficulty is the crossed term X_E = ⟨x1 ⊗ y2|W_N ⊗ P^A|x2 ⊗ y1⟩. A bound
   |X_E|² ≤ c²·(diagonal terms) with any c independent of N is equivalent to EP for every N. Crossed Cauchy–Schwarz
   for W_N ⊗ P^A alone is false already at N = 0 (take x1 = y1). So any induction must move slack from the symmetric
   part of the last copy to its antisymmetric part.

## Approaches that provably fail

- **One-copy reductions.**
  - The dephasing condition is false at N = 2 (`contraction-notes/`, exact certificate).
  - The site-contraction inequality with constant 1 − t is false at N = 3 (same folder).
  - Canonical choices of the compression direction fail at N = 2 (`compression-notes/`).
  - Averages over a basis or over contractions certify only t ≤ 1/3.
  - Single-copy squeeze pencils (`tangent-class/`). At a thick negative minimiser every one-copy pencil
    Q_s − λN_s is PSD at the same λ = m. For general rank-two operators, the sign-free version of this (no
    common PSD shift in [1 − 3t, 0)) is false at N = 3 at every Werner parameter we tried, from t = 0.335 to
    t = ½: there are thick operators with all three pencils positive definite there. On tangent planes even
    the per-copy version is false (exactly, at N = 3), and so is every relaxation we tried: tangent operators can be
    squeeze minima at every copy, and can have all pencils positive definite at one common λ = −0.01 (N = 3, 4).
- **Fixed-size convex certificates.**
  - F_qubit ⊗ W is not decomposable already at N = 1 (best value −1/7).
  - Twirled PPT and symmetric-extension relaxations fail by N = 3.
  - A PSD operator certificate on the four-slot form of 2 fails at N = 1 (−√3).
- **Bounded Schmidt number.** Every state of Schmidt number at most two has ‖σ^Γ‖₁ ≤ 2 Tr σ. But
  ‖(ρ^{⊗N})^Γ‖₁ / Tr = (17/15)^N > 2 from N = 6. So ρ^{⊗N} has Schmidt number greater than two from six copies on. No
  cone generated by filterings of one copy, in any output dimension, contains ρ^{⊗6}.
- **Coarse-graining onto known theorems.** Writing q_N as a positive combination of two- or three-block forms (the
  dimension-free two-copy theorem of Bharti–Gajjala–Haug, Tomar's three-copy theorem) plus nonnegative terms is
  infeasible at N = 4. The N singleton coefficients −1/2 cannot be supplied with total weight at most one.
- **Single-vector anchors** (`support-and-projector-notes/`, Section 5).
  - The Schur certificate of the product-ray theorem extends to "universal anchors". Random vectors sampled at
    N ≤ 4 were anchors, but anchors are not generic: for every N ≥ 3 both the anchors and the non-anchors contain
    nonempty open sets (Section 5 there; the W-plane and its neighbours contain no anchor).
  - Single excitations over a product are not anchors, and badness passes to tensor products
    (T_{u⊗g} = T_u ⊗ T_g with T_g ∋ 1).
  - So arguments that try to force a universal anchor into the range of a negative witness, after doubling or
    flagging, are circular.
- **Code concatenation.** Inner blocks have a positive semidefinite logical Gram, so concatenated projectors are
  automatically positive. This is the known products-of-planes class.
- **Relative anchors outside the single-excitation sector** (added 4 November 2026; compare
  `single-excitation-range/`, Lemma 11 and Conjecture 12). Restricting the anchor certificate to a charge sector
  of a local U(1) works numerically for single excitations and their tangent planes. As a general principle it fails:
  - **Same sector, qubit corners.** Take rows and columns both in a full two-letter subspace, for example rows and
    columns in H_4 at four copies, or in {0,1}^{⊗n} for the charge that counts letter 2. Then the compression has
    negative eigenvalues at random vectors, for example −3.2 × 10^{−2} (rows and columns in H_4, n = 4) and
    −1.3 × 10^{−2}, −2.3 × 10^{−3} ({0,1}^{⊗n}, n = 3, 4).
    The endpoint itself holds there, because two letters make the state PPT, so only the certificate fails.
  - **Different sectors.** For planes spanned by charge eigenvectors of different charges, such as span{x_2, y_1}
    with x_2 ∈ H_2 and y_1 ∈ H_1, the compression with rows in the other sector has negative eigenvalues. Examples:
    p ∈ H_1 with rows in H_2 and columns in H_1 gives −6.9 × 10^{−3} (random p) and −1.0 × 10^{−2} (W). A Dicke vector
    p ∈ H_2 with rows in H_1 and columns in H_2 gives −1.0 × 10^{−2}. All at n = 3.
  - So such planes need more than a sector-restricted anchor, and so does a general plane. General planes lie in
    no charge sector. Planes of this kind arise as fixed points of filter flows.
  - Script: `single-excitation-range/code/check_sector_anchor_failures.py`. It also re-checks that the
    single-excitation sector passes at the same sizes.

- **Weight-enumerator linear programs** (added 4 November 2026; `support-and-projector-notes/`, Section 7, and
  `support-and-projector-notes/code/lp_obstructions/`). By Theorem 14″ there, it suffices to treat rank-two projectors
  whose range is balanced. For them, the two-replica relations of quantum codes prove positivity up to eight copies
  and fail at nine. Asking the range to be t-uniform (maximally mixed marginals on any t copies) helps. The least t
  with a positive optimum, found by an exact rational simplex, is 1 for n ≤ 8; 2 at n = 9; 3 for 10–16; 4 for 17–18;
  5 for 19–23; 6 for 24–27; 7 for 28–32; and 8 at 33. So it grows linearly, roughly like n/4.3. The flag encodings we
  know give much weaker uniformity on N copies, so uniformity does not rescue the linear program.
  A three-replica relaxation helps only slightly. It twirls Π^{⊗3} over U(3) at every copy and imposes Λ³(ran Π) = 0,
  the rank-two condition. It reproduces the linear-programming bound for n ≤ 8, except at n = 7, where it gives
  0.252 against 0.232.
- **Extending the three-copy proof of Wu and Zou** (added 4 November 2026). Their proof for positive semidefinite
  rank-two operators (Wu and Zou, arXiv:2608.02647) combines nonnegatively the two-block "cut" inequalities
  z_{A∪K} + z_{A∪L} − z_A ≤ √(e_A(u) e_A(v)), with C = λP_u + μP_v, z_T = Tr(ρ^u_T ρ^v_T), e_A the linear entropy of
  the cut A, and (A, K, L) any partition of the copies into three nonempty parts. With the dilation of
  `support-and-projector-notes/` (Proposition 4′, which works for either parity of the number of added copies), the
  endpoint for every n is equivalent to positivity on positive semidefinite rank-two operators for every odd n.
  For odd n ≥ 5, however, no certificate of this kind exists, even after adding the three-copy theorem for every way
  of grouping the copies into three blocks. The obstruction comes from product pairs u = ⊗u_i, v = ⊗v_i with
  u_i ⊥ v_i on an odd set D and u_i = v_i elsewhere. The n-copy statement is an equality at every such pair. Every
  cut with |K| ≥ 2 or |L| ≥ 2, and every grouped inequality with a block of two or more copies, has slack at some such
  pair. The remaining cuts relate only the top two levels. The linear programs at n = 5, 7 confirm infeasibility.
  So a proof has to resolve every copy individually. Positive semidefinite operators at n = 5 are the first open case
  of this form, since n = 4 is covered for Hermitian operators by `four-replica-notes/`. Compare
  `four-replica-notes/NOTES.md`, Section 6: the level-by-level and cut-by-cut forms of the Plücker inequality are
  already false at n = 4, and the four-replica relaxation that is exact at n = 4 gives −7.47 × 10^{−3} at n = 5.

- **Bounded Schmidt number of the tensor powers (added 8 November 2026).** If the Schmidt number of ρ^{⊗n} grew more
  slowly than every exponential in n, ρ would be undistillable: a positive distillation rate E_D turns n copies into
  about nE_D ebits, which needs Schmidt number at least 2^{n(E_D − δ)}, and local operations cannot raise the Schmidt
  number. So "SN(ρ^{⊗n}) = 2^{o(n)}" would give NPT bound entanglement directly. It cannot hold for any entangled ρ.
  The entanglement cost of every entangled state is positive (Yang, Horodecki, Horodecki and Synak-Radtke, Phys. Rev.
  Lett. 95, 190501 (2005)), and log SN(σ) ≥ E_F(σ), so SN(ρ^{⊗n}) ≥ 2^{n E_C(ρ)}. This matches the finite evidence in
  `compression-notes/`, Section 9: ρ ⊗ ρ and ρ^{⊗3} have Schmidt number 2, but decompositions with at most one
  antisymmetric factor per term must stop at six copies, where ρ^{⊗n} puts weight 3n/5 on antisymmetric copies.
- **KL₁ through the first copy (added 8 November 2026).** For filters a, b : C³ → C³ ⊗ C³ write the two-copy Werner
  operator as W₁ ⊗ W₂ and expand W₁ = ½P_S + (3/2)P_A on the first copy. The P_S part is a mixture of one-copy
  filterings (P_S = 6∫|vv⟩⟨vv| dv), so its contribution lies in K₁(3); the P_A part is not. Averaging over planes V of
  the first copy, W₁ = E_V[P_S^{(V)} + (9/2)|s_V⟩⟨s_V|] (s_V the singlet of V ⊗ V), so even combining both parts plane
  by plane leaves, on each V ⊗ V, a two-qubit Werner operator with singlet fraction 3/5 > ½, which is entangled.
  A decomposition that factors through the first copy would need W₁ to be separable, and it is not. So, like the
  dimension bound, this shows that a proof of KL₁ has to use the 3 × 3 output bottleneck itself.
## What remains

A proof has to keep the crossing in 2 and use rank two in an essential way. The certificates it needs must be
non-decomposable and of unbounded Schmidt number, yet compatible with tensor powers. No such tensor-stable class is
known; its existence is equivalent to the problem (compare Müller-Hermes, Reeb and Wolf, J. Math. Phys. 57, 015202
(2016), on tensor-stable positive maps). Numerically, no counterexample has been found in any search we know of. That
includes:
- our searches;
- tensor-network searches to 64 copies;
- factored searches at 10 and 12 copies;
- adversarial searches over single-excitation planes, where anchors fail.

## The map route (Section 7 of `whole-problem-routes/`)

The second route asks for positivity of every tensor power of 𝒫_{3/4}(X) = ¾ Tr(X) I − ¼(X + Xᵀ) on qutrits. It is proved
for six copies, on real planes for every n, on permutation-symmetric inputs up to 21 copies, on separable inputs and on
inputs of bond dimension two for every n. A failure would need seven or more copies at which both the input and the
output have full support. The following do not reach every n (added 7 November 2026).

- **Decomposability, and every certificate that unrolls into it.** 𝒫_a^{⊗n} is not decomposable from n = 3 for any
  a < 0.7562 (Proposition 7.3), and for every a < 1 some tensor power is not decomposable, by a theorem of Müller-Hermes
  (a map all of whose tensor powers are decomposable is completely positive or completely copositive; J. Math. Phys.
  59, 102203 (2018), arXiv:1805.11570). Suppose a certificate writes C^{⊗n} as local compressions of C^{⊗m} ⊗ (separable),
  with m < n, plus decomposable terms. Substituting the certificates of the smaller powers repeatedly ends at
  C^{⊗2}, which is decomposable, so C^{⊗n} would be decomposable. This rules out "thin reductions": a compression onto
  a plane at one copy is a separable factor times C^{⊗(n−1)} (Lemma 7.7).
- **Two-replica linear programs.** The shadow inequalities Tr(Z Φ(Z)) ≥ 0 for products Φ of completely positive maps,
  together with the pure-state identities Tr(Z Z^{T_S}) = Tr Z_S² and a classical ancilla bit (so that the problem
  is a diagonal one), give exactly the decomposability bound: −3/832 at n = 3, the PPT state of Remark 7.15.
- **Fixed-level symmetric extensions.** The second level is exact at a = ¾ for n ≤ 5, but its threshold ℓ_n increases
  (0.6036, 0.648, 0.686, 0.720 for n = 2, …, 5, with increments shrinking only like n^{−1/2}). At six copies it still
  certifies a = ¾ (Theorem 7.32), but in floating point only with t ≈ 0.563·4^{−6}, no longer the product value, so it
  appears to have started to lose ground. It probably fails at a = ¾ from about n = 7, and no fixed level is expected to work for every n.
- **Word expansions.** 2C_{3/4} = M + P_an, with M = I − ½|Ω⟩⟨Ω| the Werner endpoint operator. But M ⊗ P_an is not
  positive on Schmidt-rank-two vectors (least value −1/6), and M ⊗ P_an ⊗ P_an is not block positive (−1/24). So
  neither 2-positivity of 𝒫_{3/4}^{⊗2} nor positivity of the powers splits into endpoint statements term by term.
- **Second-order (local) arguments.** From a critical point, one-copy squeezes move the value monotonically, with an
  exact formula and a closed form for the trace of the squeeze Hessian (Proposition 7.23). But at the minimiser of a
  hypothetical failure every second-order condition holds automatically, because there λ < (a − ½)^n while every
  one-copy contraction has normalised value at least (a − ½)^{n−1}. One-copy statements that would exclude thick local
  minima at every value are not expected to hold, as for the endpoint form (see `orbit-notes/`). The thick critical
  points found by random search at three and four copies are all saddles along a one-copy squeeze (Observation 7.25),
  but that is evidence about saddles, not about minima.

- **The thickness bonus by induction (added 7 November 2026).** Conjecture TC (λ_min ≥ ∏_s(¼ + ½τ_s)) is proved at two
  and three copies (Proposition 7.28, Theorem 7.29), but it does not pass from n − 1 to n copies in any obvious way.
  Conditioning on a copy can lose all the thickness: the three-copy input ½[e₁(e₁e₁ + e₂e₃) + e₂(e₁e₂ + e₃e₃)] has
  τ = ¼ at copies 2 and 3, while every conditional vector is planar (rank-two marginals). Peeling the thin first copy as
  in Proposition 7.8 then gives only 1/32, below the TC value 9/256 (true value 0.0774). Nor is there a form that
  survives an ancilla. If the input is (1/√3)Σ_i |i⟩ ⊗ e_i^{⊗n} and the ancilla is measured in the basis |i⟩, every
  one-copy marginal is I/3, but the output is block diagonal with least eigenvalue 4^{−n}/3, exactly what the product
  inputs e_i^{⊗n} give, so no bonus survives. A proof of
  TC therefore has to treat all copies at once, as the certificate of Theorem 7.29 does.
- **Linear bonuses.** The best constant k in λ_min ≥ 4^{−n}(1 + kΣ_sτ_s) is 5 at two copies (exact, attained at the
  maximally entangled vector) and 5 to search accuracy at three and four copies (attained at e^{⊗(n−2)} ⊗ Ω̂). A linear
  bound implies TC only if k ≥ (3/n)((5/3)^n − 1), which is 98/27 at n = 3 but 5.04 > 5 at n = 4. So certificates for a
  linear bonus, like that of Theorem 7.29, cannot give TC from four copies on; there the product form has to be
  certified directly. Without a variable for the least eigenvector, simple linear stand-ins for τ_s fail already at two
  copies.

- **Exponential bonuses and product test vectors (added 7 November 2026).** Against real product test vectors the
  bonus holds for every n and every input, in the stronger form 4^{−n}3^{2Σ_sτ_s} (Proposition 7.30, by Jensen's
  inequality). It cannot be extended to entangled test vectors in that form: the singlet pair of Observation 7.26 gives
  (151 − √6805)/1536 ≈ 0.0446 < 4^{−4}3^{8/3}, and no bound 4^{−n}exp(κΣ_sτ_s) with a single κ gives Conjecture TC from
  four copies on (κ ≤ 1.83 there, κ ≥ 2 needed near thin inputs). Averaging over a separable decomposition extends the
  bound to every separable input against every test vector (Proposition 7.33), but no convexity argument goes further:
  at the same singlet pair the bottom test vector is balanced, every separable input gives at least (¾)⁴3^{−4/3} ≈ 0.0731
  there, and the true value is 0.0446, so 𝒫_{3/4}^{⊗4}(xxᵀ) dominates no separable image.
- **Peeling the least eigenvalue (added 7 November 2026).** Splitting 𝒫_{3/4} = ¼D + 𝒫_{1/2} at one copy reduces
  Conjecture TC for mixed inputs exactly to a bound on the vertex map 𝒫_{1/2} ⊗ 𝒫_{3/4}^{⊗(n−1)} at a suitable copy
  (Remark 7.31). That bound fails at a fixed copy (0.947 at n = 2), and the simpler bound with the least eigenvalue of the
  marginal's output in place of the thickness product fails even at the best copy (0.954 at n = 2). With all τ_s = 0 the
  vertex bound is real 2-positivity of 𝒫_{3/4}^{⊗(n−1)}, which is equivalent to the whole route; its first open case
  (two copies) is not certified by the second level of the hierarchy, even with the vertex copy restricted to a plane
  (−8.3·10⁻³; full qutrit −4.6·10⁻³).
- **Fixed levels of the hierarchy, again (added 8 November 2026).** The second-level thresholds ℓ_n (Remark 7.15) are
  0.648, 0.686, 0.720 for n = 3, 4, 5, and our six-copy solves put ℓ₆ in (0.745, 0.75) (the value at a = 0.745 is
  −2.9·10⁻⁴, and Theorem 7.32 holds at 3/4). Their increments shrink, which could suggest a limit near 0.9, so that one
  n-independent second-level certificate at some a < 1 would settle the route. The same extrapolation fails for the
  first level. The decomposability thresholds of Proposition 7.3 (exact linear program) are 0.756, 0.807, 0.840, 0.864
  for n = 3, …, 6, which extrapolates the same way to about 0.92, yet they tend to 1 by the theorem of Müller-Hermes
  (0.928 at n = 12, with increment ratios rising toward 1). So there is no evidence that any fixed level works for every
  n at a fixed a < 1. The optimal six-copy dual point just below ℓ₆ is spread over many spin blocks (no block carries
  more than 6% of its trace), so it does not suggest an explicit witness for every n either. All of this is floating
  point.
- **Descent at thick critical points (added 8 November 2026).** By Corollary 7.10 the route would follow if the least
  value at every n were attained at an input that is thin somewhere, that is, if no input thick at every copy were a
  local minimum. In our searches thick critical points escape in three different ways. If the bottom eigenvalue is
  degenerate, a global squeeze splits it at first order (six copies, rotation-invariant inputs). If the partner test
  vector is not itself optimal as an input, moving the input toward the partner's better input lowers the value (six
  copies). At a mutual minimum, where each vector is the best response to the other, only the four-copy singlet pair
  of Observation 7.26 is known. Its five descent directions (one spin-2 multiplet, Hessian eigenvalue −0.002845) are a
  global squeeze ⊗_s(I + εQ) of the input, the same symmetric traceless Q at every copy, together with a response of the
  test vector that has a two-copy part (31% of its squared norm). No one-copy move descends there, not even independent
  one-copy filters on the input and on the test vector. From the partner input every global squeeze goes up. So a
  descent proof would need two-copy moves chosen from the operator itself, and we have no rule for them. Along the four
  global squeezes we followed, the value at the first input falls monotonically from 11.42·4^{−4} to the product value
  4^{−4}, reached at an input supported on planes. Best-response iteration from random starts (n ≤ 5) and from inputs
  with total J_z = 0 (n ≤ 6) only finds thin mutual minima; from rotation-invariant starts it finds the singlet pair
  (n = 4) and a degenerate point (n = 6). At eight copies, where two-copy responses span only a small part of the space
  and the descent pattern would be a real test, the same iteration from rotation-invariant starts ended at thin pairs
  in two of three starts (values 4^{−8} and 8·4^{−8}); the third had not converged after an hour. Summed over the five
  global squeezes, the second variation at the four-copy pair is −0.029, but bounding its resolvent term through
  (W − λ)⁺ ⪰ (λ_max − λ)^{−1} on the complement of the test vector gives the upper bound +0.127, so closed-form bounds
  of this kind cannot show descent. All of this is floating point.
- **One-copy compressions (added 8 November 2026).** Observation 7.25 suggested an induction: for every test vector y,
  some compression of one copy onto a plane does not raise g(y) = λ_min(𝒫_{3/4}^{⊗n}(yyᵀ))/|y|², and thin vectors are
  controlled by fewer copies. This fails at four copies. At the partner vector of Observation 7.26 every one-copy
  compression, in any direction and with the input re-optimised, raises g from 11.418·4^{−4} to 11.733·4^{−4}, and
  since g is multiplicative on products, so does every one-copy compression of y ⊗ y at eight copies. At
  three copies, the rules that compress along the middle or the smallest eigenvector of a one-copy marginal already
  fail. So no compression of one copy onto a plane, chosen from y alone, works at every input
  (`whole-problem-routes/code/compression_rules/`). Compressing two copies at once survives the same tests (an
  adversarial search at four copies, and the six-copy singlet minimum), but at the minimiser of a failure every such
  move would have to raise g as well, so a statement of this kind is a reformulation of the problem, not a route.
- **The sharp rank-two bound (added 8 November 2026).** For C ⪰ 0 of rank at most two, the endpoint form satisfies
  2ⁿ q_n(½, C) ≥ 2 Tr C² − (Tr C)² at three copies (Wu and Zou, arXiv:2608.02647, Theorem 1.3); for odd n this inequality would give the
  endpoint by the odd-projector reduction of `support-and-projector-notes/`. At five copies, nine adversarial runs
  (random, near qubit-supported and near product starts, gradient descent on the exact form) found no violation; the
  minima are 0 to rounding. Its rank-one case holds for every n and every local dimension: for a unit vector u with
  marginal purities p_K, the symmetry p_K = p_{K^c} gives 2ⁿ q_n(½, |u⟩⟨u|) = Σ_K (−1)^{n−|K|} 2^{|K|} p_K = Σ_S Sh(S),
  where Sh(S) = Σ_{J⊆S} (−1)^{|J|} p_J ≥ 0 is Rains's shadow (positivity of tensor powers of the reduction map), and
  Sh(∅) = 1. The rank-two case is the whole difficulty, and two natural relaxations of it fail. Rains's shadows of C
  together with those of its purification by a qubit, and the purity relations of that purification, allow the value
  −6 at three copies (−60 at five), so purity profiles alone cannot prove it. The simplest uniform Cauchy–Schwarz
  certificate, with the two-replica operator of the identity 2ⁿq_n(C) − 2TrC² + (TrC)² = Tr[(C ⊗ C)𝒟_n] as its own
  kernel, already fails at three copies. In the form B_n(u, v) ≤ √(A_n(u)A_n(v)) for the two eigenvectors (B_n and A_n
  are weighted sums of antisymmetric-sector weights over the levels), the levels are genuinely coupled: at five copies
  each of the level-wise bounds 3p₃ − p₂ ≤ √(a₂(u)a₂(v)) and 3p₅ − p₄ ≤ √(a₄(u)a₄(v)) fails (by 0.625 and 0.094), at
  points where the coupled bound holds with equality and the two levels compensate with the weight 10. Nor does Wu and
  Zou's method extend. Their two lemmas (‖Tr_A X‖² + ‖Tr_B X‖² ≤ ‖X‖₁² + |Tr X|², and the exterior-square bound
  ‖X‖₁² − ‖X‖₂² ≤ √(e(u)e(v)) for X = Tr_K|u⟩⟨v|), applied to every traced set K and every bipartition of the rest, our
  first-order bound of `first-order-bound/` over every partition into three or more blocks, and even its conjectured
  sharper form for traceless operators, combined linearly with the nonnegativity of all sector weights and one
  weighted Cauchy–Schwarz step, give a certificate at three copies (the linear program returns exactly Wu and Zou's)
  but none at five copies (infeasible). The dual of that program shows how far off it is. It returns pseudo-profiles
  (cross purities c_S and purities P_S) that obey every linearised constraint but exceed the bound by 5.65, and the
  excess stays at 4.03 after adding four further families of valid constraints: the dimension bounds
  P_S ≥ 3^{−min(|S|, n−|S|)}, c_S ≤ P_S, least-eigenvalue lower bounds on one-copy cross purities, and the Uhlmann bounds
  c_{S^c} ≤ 3^{min(|S|, n−|S|)} c_S. So the five-copy case needs inequalities of a different kind
  (`whole-problem-routes/code/sharp_rank_two/`, floating point; the linear programs are exact up to solver tolerance).
- **Zero-free regions (added 8 November 2026).** For C of rank at most two let
  f_C(t₁, …, t_n) = Σ_J (∏_{s∈J} t_s) ‖Tr_J C‖², so the endpoint is f_C(−½, …, −½) ≥ 0. In our tests the diagonal
  polynomial f_C(t, …, t) has no root in |t| < ½ (random C, n ≤ 5: smallest root modulus 0.71 to 0.80), although it is
  usually not real-rooted. A complex strengthening would allow a Lee–Yang argument through Asano contractions:
  f_C(t) ≠ 0 whenever every |t_s| < ½. It survives adversarial search at two and three copies (the smallest root
  ratio is exactly ½, attained at the product zeros; on balanced projectors at three copies it is 0.70), but it is
  false. For a real plane with z = (u + iv)/√2, put D(t) = f_{zz*}(t) and X(t) = Σ_J (∏_{s∈J} t_s)⟨Tr_J zz*, Tr_J z̄zᵀ⟩.
  Then f_P = 2(D + X) and f_J = 2(D − X) for P = uuᵀ + vvᵀ and J = uvᵀ − vuᵀ, and the endpoint for planes is
  |X| ≤ D at t = −½ (Corollary F of `whole-problem-routes/`). On the complex disk this bound fails from four copies on:
  at four copies there is a plane with |X/D| = 1.62 at t_s = t, |t| = 0.499 (five copies: 3.4). Fusion
  (Proposition F) multiplies X/D, so by the open mapping theorem some power of that plane, at a common t with |t| < ½,
  has X/D real and below −1, and then C = C_z + αC_z̄ with a suitable α > 0 has f_C = 0 there. The violating plane is
  qubit-supported (every one-copy marginal of P/2 has spectrum (½, ½, 0)) and J = uvᵀ − vuᵀ has all its partial traces
  zero, as for GHZ-type planes; on the real segment it is harmless (q₄(P) = ¼ at t = −½). So complex zero-freeness
  fails already in the qubit sector, where the real endpoint is trivial (for qubits every coefficient (1 − 2t)^{|K|} of
  the Fourier form is nonnegative). The endpoint holds, if at all, only for real parameters
  (`whole-problem-routes/code/zero_free/`, floating point; the four-copy value is recomputed in plain numpy).
- **Self-similarity (added 8 November 2026).** One finite identity would settle the every-n question. If for some
  m > k the Choi matrix C_a^{⊗m} of 𝒫_a^{⊗m} were a positive combination of local filterings (A ⊗ B)C_a^{⊗k}(A ⊗ B)^*
  plus a separable operator, then 𝒫_a^{⊗mN} would be a sum of filtered copies of 𝒫_a^{⊗K}, K ≤ kN, tensored with
  entanglement-breaking maps, and strong induction (from positivity below m copies) would make 𝒫_a tensor-stable
  positive; at any a < 1 this gives NPT bound entanglement. For m = 2, k = 1 it fails numerically at every a < 1 we
  tried. Fits of C_a ⊗ C_a by 40 filterings of C_a and 120 products leave relative residuals 6.2·10⁻³, 2.8·10⁻²,
  4.9·10⁻², 6.0·10⁻² and 0.105 at a = 0.99, 0.95, 0.9, 0.85 and 0.75, against 7·10⁻⁵ for a random element of the cone
  and 4·10⁻⁵ at a = 1, where C₁ ⊗ C₁ is separable. The residual grows like 0.6(1 − a), which suggests an obstruction
  already at first order in 1 − a (heuristically: on the vectors with a factor φ at one copy the first-order part of
  the target is negative definite, and a filtering of fewer copies can reproduce it only by mapping the φ of every copy
  to a φ). This is the map's analogue of the uncompressed test in `compression-notes/` (ρ ⊗ ρ is not in the cone of
  filterings of one Werner copy). Larger m, k were not tested (`whole-problem-routes/code/self_similarity/`, floating
  point).
- **Classical forms of the value (added 8 November 2026).** For every Hermitian X,
  𝒫_{3/4}(X) = ∫ 3⟨v|X|v⟩ [(I − vvᵀ) − ¼vvᵀ] dv, with v uniform on the real unit sphere. So 𝒫_{3/4} = 𝒩 − ¼ℳ with
  ℳ(X) = (Tr(X)I + 2 Sym X)/5 and 𝒩 = D − ℳ, both entanglement breaking, and for every n and all unit x, y

      ⟨y|𝒫_{3/4}^{⊗n}(xx*)|y⟩ = E[(−¼)^N],

  where v is drawn from the product measurement 3^n|⟨v|x⟩|² dv and N counts the copies at which the binary test
  {v_s v_sᵀ, I − v_s v_sᵀ} on y returns v_s. This is the collision form of Remark 7.6 with the test vector read out by a
  binary test instead of the design, which lowers r from 3/2 to ¼. Independent hits would make the value positive (a
  negative factor needs a hit probability above 4/5), and aligned real products give 4^{−n} with independent hits at
  3/5. Every hit probability, also conditional on any information about the other copies, lies in [1/5, 3/5], because
  (Tr X/5) I ⪯ ℳ(X) ⪯ (3 Tr X/5) I for X ⪰ 0 and ℳ, 𝒩 and D are entanglement breaking. These constraints do not
  suffice. The exchangeable law on three copies whose conditional hit probabilities are 3/5, 1/5 and 3/5 (given 0, 1
  and 2 hits at the other copies) has E[(−¼)^N] = −13/1472, although each two-copy block, conditioned on the third copy,
  has the positive values 1/16 and 23/48. For 3 ≤ n ≤ 16 the worst laws of this kind stay negative (from n = 4 on: a first
  hit likely, every later one unlikely). At the thick extremal pairs the hits are anti-correlated (pair covariances down to
  −0.034 at the four-copy singlet pair), and the values stay high because the hit probabilities there are 1/3. The
  spin-sector form V = (7/12)^n Σ_S (−6/7)^{|S|} c_S, with c_S = ⟨Π₂^{⊗S}(σ_S), Π₂^{⊗S}(ρ_S)⟩ (Π₂ the projection onto
  symmetric traceless matrices, ρ and σ the states of x and y), behaves the same way. Balanced inputs have no one-copy
  terms, and k-uniform inputs no terms with 1 ≤ |S| ≤ k, but the bounds |c_S| ≤ ‖ρ_S‖₂‖σ_S‖₂ do not control the rest, and
  the balanced inputs produced by the reductions of `real-balanced-reduction/` carry flag copies whose two-copy
  marginals are far from maximally mixed. So a proof has to use quantum structure beyond one-copy conditional bounds and
  two-copy blocks, at every n (`whole-problem-routes/code/hit_form/`, log in its `logs/`; the three-copy law is exact,
  the rest floating point).

What a proof of this route needs is a global inequality: some way to compare the value at a thick minimiser with the
values of its contractions or compressions that does not hold trivially at every critical point. None of the tools
above provides one.
